Does the marked verb express a stance, judgment, reaction, or attempted response to an occurrence named by the marked noun?
Answer Y when the verb reacts to, evaluates, supports, opposes, or tries to affect the noun's occurrence, so the sentence presents both occurrences.
Answer N when the noun is merely the object changed, canceled, used, owned, or described by the verb, or a topic of a standing relationship.
In “leaders opposed the cancellation,” the answer is Y.
In “officials criticized the use of force,” the answer is Y.
In “officials canceled the contract,” the answer is N.
In “the parties disagreed over policy,” the answer is N.
