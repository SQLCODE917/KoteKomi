Decide whether the marked noun should head its own entry in a complete inventory of the particular occurrences expressed by this sentence.
Answer E when the noun itself names a particular action, communication, decision, proceeding, change, meeting, evaluation, election, conflict beginning, or other bounded occurrence that the sentence asserts or presupposes.
Answer N when the noun names a person, role, organization, place, object, document, product, policy, program, topic, purpose, method, enduring condition, standing relationship, or general activity rather than its own particular occurrence.
Background, nested, planned, hypothetical, conditional, and negated occurrences still count.
When a light verb merely helps an event noun, the noun is E: in “officials conducted negotiations,” negotiations is E.
When a content verb says what happened to an object, the object is N: in “officials published a report,” report is N.
An occurrence used as a time reference still counts: in “after the election, officials met,” election is E.
A purpose does not count: in “an effort to reduce costs,” effort is N.
Judge this use of the marked noun only.
Return exactly one character: E or N.
